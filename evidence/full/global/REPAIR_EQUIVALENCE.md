# Characteristic-zero repair target: an equivalence and two obstructions

23 September 2026. This is a new analysis of the all-degrees route, not
a solution of the conjecture. No previous frozen output was modified.
Primary source: [Ghosh, arXiv:2501.09272v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09272v2),
Proposition 3.3 and Sections 4.1–4.2. The source's regular-sequence
reformulation and height input are stated explicitly below; the latter
comes from its prequel and is not reproved here.

## 1. The exact contraction target is equivalent to the missing CA step

Let R=k[x_1,...,x_m], S=R[t], and set

    I=(F_1,...,F_m), F_i=t f_i+g_i,
    J=(f_1,...,f_m), D=I_2([f;g]).

Assume:

1. A linear polynomial L=t+ell, ell in R, completes I to an ideal
   of height m+1 in S.
2. Every minimal prime of D has height m-1 and R/D has no embedded
   associated primes. The paper obtains this from the expected-height
   determinantal Cohen–Macaulay theorem and the next height input.
3. J is proper and height(J)>=m-1.

Then

    I intersect R = D    if and only if    height(J)=m.           (A)

This is an algebraic equivalence, not just an implication. In the
paper's special family the right-hand side is exactly CA in the lower
degree, by the regular-sequence reformulation. The first hypothesis
follows from the assumed upper-degree CA statement. Thus directly
proving the proposed contraction identity would constitute the missing
descent theorem; it is not a formally weaker replacement lemma.

**Proof, height(J)=m direction.** At a minimal prime p of D, the
height condition ensures some f_l is a unit in R_p. Eliminating t
using F_l gives

    I S_p intersect R_p = D R_p.

The other minors are generated by the minors involving column l,
using the usual determinant identity and the unit f_l. The kernel
of R/D -> S/I vanishes after localization at every minimal prime.
Since R/D has no embedded associated primes, that kernel is zero.

**Proof, converse.** If height(J)=m-1, choose a minimal prime p of
J of that height. As D subset J, hypothesis 2 makes p a minimal
prime of D as well. Some g_l is a unit in R_p: otherwise all F_i
would lie in pS, and I+(L) would be contained in pS+(L), of height
m, contradicting hypothesis 1.

The local ring A=R_p/D_p is Artinian. Every f_i belongs to its
nilradical, since f_i is in p. Therefore

    F_l = t f_l+g_l

is a unit in A[t]: its constant coefficient is a unit and its
slope is nilpotent. It follows that I S_p=S_p (because D subset I).
Localization commutes with contraction, so (I intersect R)_p=R_p,
whereas D_p is proper. Thus the contraction identity fails.

The proof does not use Proposition 3.3 or its generator equality.
It pinpoints the geometric loss: lower-degree CA components appear
in the determinant locus but are completely lost on the affine
chart parametrized by finite t. Nilpotent polynomial units describe
that loss exactly.

There is no logical objection to proving the left side of (A) by
a new argument. But any such proof must do the full work of
excluding the lower-degree CA configurations under the upper-degree
hypothesis. Calling it a routine elimination repair would conceal
that burden.

## 2. Generic reducedness already fails in the genuine CA family

Take the actual n=4 setup with all three marked indices equal to
the identity choice. In R=Q[x,y,z], write

    a=e3=xyz, b=e2=xy+xz+yz, c=e1=x+y+z.

The genuine coefficients of the truncated upper system are

    f=(a,b,c), g=(0,a,b), F=(ta,tb+a,tc+b),
    D=(a²,ab,b²-ca).

Set-theoretically, a²=0 gives a=0 and then b²=0 gives b=0.
Hence the radical of D is (xy,xz,yz); the three coordinate-axis
primes are the minimal primes. Consider p=(x,y), where z is a
unit. In the p-adic order filtration, a has initial form zxy of
degree two, while the only order-two generator of D has initial
form

    z²(x²+xy+y²).

The other displayed generators have orders three and four. The
initial form zxy is not a scalar multiple of the displayed quadratic
over Q(z). Therefore a is not in D_p, but a² is in D_p. The
Artinian determinantal quotient has a genuine nonzero nilpotent.

Thus a blanket claim that the relevant determinantal ideals are
generically reduced in characteristic zero is false even in this
small, authentic CA family. This is stronger evidence than an
unrelated abstract ring example. It is not a counterexample to
Lemma 4.5: here c=f_3 is a unit at p, so this is a good component
for contraction. A reducedness claim restricted only to hypothetical
components containing every f_i remains unproved and targets
precisely the unresolved lower-degree CA locus.

The exact script `actual_nilpotents.sing` also checks that contraction
does hold in this example despite the nilpotents. Therefore reducedness
is neither an available blanket hypothesis nor a necessary condition
for the successful cases of the argument.

## 3. The surrounding algebraic hypotheses cannot repair cancellation

The following characteristic-zero model keeps more of the paper's
structure than the earlier audit counterexample. It is explicitly
not claimed to be a CA root configuration.

Let R=Q[u,v,z,s], S=R[t], and choose

    f=(u4, uvs, v², z),
    g=(-u5, s4, 2v3, 3z²),
    F_i=t f_i+g_i.

The f degrees are 4,3,2,1, the g degrees are 5,4,3,2, and the
upper degrees are 5,4,3,2, exactly the descending pattern used in
the proof. Also g_1=-u f_1, the same first-column relation satisfied
by the actual elementary-symmetric construction.

The ideal J=(f) has global generator number four. At p=(u,v,z),
s is a unit and

    J_p=(u4,uv,v²,z),     mu(J_p)=4.

These monomials are minimal local generators. Meanwhile g_2=s4
is a unit, so the three minors involving column 2 generate D_p.
Their extra factors are units at p, giving exactly

    D_p=(u4,v²,z).

For example the relevant factors, up to signs and units, are
u4(s3+u²v), v²(2uv²-s3), z(3uvz-s3). Thus

    A=R_p/D_p = Q(s)[u,v]_(u,v)/(u4,v²).

The element uv is nonzero and square-zero. In A[t],

    F_2=s4+uvs t

is a polynomial unit with inverse s^(-4)*(1-uv t/s3). Therefore
I S_p=S_p and contraction strictly exceeds D at this component,
despite the global/local generator equality mu(J)=mu(J_p)=4.

The upper equations are a regular sequence: modulo t, their
constants generate (u5,s4,v3,z²), which is primary to the maximal
ideal. Hence I+(t) has height five, forcing height(I)=4.

The determinantal ideal has expected height three and is therefore
Cohen–Macaulay. One can prove the height lower bound as follows.
At a minimal prime containing all f_i it follows from
radical(J)=(u,v,z). At any other minimal prime, a unit f_i eliminates
t, and the regularity of F gives height three. The general maximal-
minor bound supplies the reverse inequality.

Even all the actual-form last linear equations are available:

    L_r=u+v+z+s+t-6r,  r in {u,v,z,s,t,0}.

Each I+(L_r) has height five. This is verified exactly in
`descending_degree_model.sing`; the proof can also be read from
F_1=u4(t-u), F_3=v²(t+2v), F_4=z(t+3z), and
F_2=s(uvt+s3). If u,v are both nonzero, normalize u=t=1;
then v=-1/2, z is 0 or -1/3, and s is 0 or satisfies s3=1/2.
None of the six L_r vanishes: vanishing would prescribe one of
finitely many rational values of s whose cube is not 1/2. If
u=0 or v=0, then s=0 and the remaining displayed factors give
the same conclusion directly. The t-axis is also harmless.

Thus the complete-intersection upper hypothesis, all specified
linear completions, descending degrees, local/global generator
equality, first-column relation, and determinantal Cohen–Macaulayness
do not imply the cancellation sought. Additional derivative-specific
identities are indispensable.

## 4. The exact missing all-degrees bridge

For the special elementary-symmetric family, let m be the number
of old root variables. Writing the actual F_i as t f_i+g_i gives

    f_i = Phi_(j_i) e_(m+1-i),
    g_i = Phi_(j_i) e_(m+2-i) - x_(j_i) f_i,

with x_(identity)=0 and e_(m+1)=0. At a lower-degree CA root
configuration, the values g_i are proportional to the preceding
derivative evaluated at the root chosen for derivative i.

A successful repair must rule out an all-f-vanishing component of
the determinant locus with some g_i a unit, using these particular
linked derivative identities. The general commutative algebra and
degree pattern do not do so. By (A), uniformly ruling out precisely
these components under upper-degree CA is equivalent to the desired
descent. Together with known unbounded good degrees it would solve
the full conjecture, but no proof of that bridge has been obtained.

The global minimal-generator theorem alone cannot supply it:
essential generators do not imply expected height, as the model
above demonstrates with the correct degrees. Likewise generic
rank or generic finite-fiber statements in a Goncharoff/root-incidence
formulation do not exclude a positive-dimensional special zero
fiber; one needs a uniform properness or special-fiber theorem.

This analysis leaves a precise target rather than a repaired proof.
It supplies no solution of unrestricted degree 20 or of full CA.
