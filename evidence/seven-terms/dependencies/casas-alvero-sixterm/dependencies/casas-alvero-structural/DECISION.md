# Research decision

The new round produced a substantive extension: a single, fixed-degree Frobenius obstruction proves exclusion for the moving family X^(p+7)+a X^(p+3)+c X^3+d X in all but finitely many prime characteristics. The earlier result treated p=13 and its prime-power lifts; this result varies the underlying prime as well. The exceptional set has an explicit resultant definition and a fast exact membership-exclusion test. It is not fully classified.

The strongest reason to retain the result is its uniformity. Running more independent prime computations would not provide the same theorem. The short modular Bezout identity provides a compact, independently reconstructed certificate for the load-bearing finiteness step.

The result remains a support obstruction. It resolves no unrestricted new degree of the Casas–Alvero conjecture, does not classify all three-coefficient exceptional cases, and is not yet shown to meet a specialist journal's significance threshold. Internal audit and portable arithmetic checks do not settle that threshold or historical priority.

The prior-art investigation resolved the missing-thesis gap. Older singleton and two-support methods account for the boundary classifications and halve the next six-term support frontier. They do not, in the checked exact applications, subsume the characteristic-13 seed or the new moving-family theorem. The candidate contribution is the specific uniform construction and its consequences, not valuation reduction, Hasse lifting, resultant methods, or the general Frobenius observation.

Decision: retain the uniform theorem as a candidate suitable for specialist mathematical assessment, while holding publication. For further research, prioritize a stronger degree-20 conclusion or a compact classification of the moving family's genuine exceptions. More isolated good primes, a larger certificate, or more elaborate packaging would not answer the significance question. This round does not authorize or perform publication or contact with authors.
