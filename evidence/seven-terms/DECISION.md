# What the focused attack achieved

The requested attack on C is complete, subject to the assurance boundary of an unpublished internally audited mathematical proof. All six residue configurations have been excluded. The resulting seven-term lower bound is stronger than the preceding six-term result and closes the entire next sparsity case, rather than only some selected examples.

The useful mechanism is the recovery of characteristic-zero precision from the residue equations. A unit Jacobian forces certain root and coefficient deviations into 13 times the valuation ring. In the cluster branch, a high-derivative witness equation supplies that precision despite an initially multiple residue root. In one collision branch, a second application is essential before the middle derivative supplies a contradiction. These are standard valuation principles; the candidate contribution is their complete arithmetic application to the degree-20 support frontier.

This changes the assessment of the sparse problem: the seven-term target was tractable, and is now achieved. It does not establish that unrestricted degree 20, or the full Casas–Alvero conjecture, is tractable. The next coefficient-count case has not been enumerated or solved here, and a proof for all degrees would need a substantially broader mechanism. No automatic extension to that larger task has been started.

This is worth retaining as a specialist research result and presenting for mathematical and priority review. It is not yet a justified claim of a major breakthrough. The unresolved overlap with a public project's undisclosed support computations prevents historical-priority clearance. The present authorization concerns research; no publication, submission, outreach, or recurring automation was performed.
