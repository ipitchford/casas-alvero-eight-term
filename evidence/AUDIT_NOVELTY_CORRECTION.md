# Update to the sparse theorem's novelty assessment

23 September 2026, during the authorized continuation.

The degree-20 conclusion “at least five nonzero terms in centered normal form” is already implied by existing arguments. It should no longer be treated as a candidate new theorem. The earlier explicit certificate and its mathematical proof remain valid.

Massri's [arXiv:1806.09561v6, Remark 7.4 and proof of Theorem 7.9](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.09561v6#S7) exclude a common root for derivative pairs (5,10) and (10,15) of a degree-20 Casas–Alvero counterexample. Together with the mean-root and prime-power restrictions in Castryck–Laterveer–Ounaïes, these leave only coefficient-index supports {4,10,19} and {10,16,19} for four total centered terms. Both fail CLO's determinant condition modulo 19. Thus the lower bound is an implicit corollary of earlier work.

The first search compared stated sparsity and recycled-root theorems but did not extract this stronger consequence from the proof of a multiplicity theorem. The extended audit found the overlap by translating shared-derivative roots into missing coefficient positions. An independent short 5-adic derivation is being recorded in the continuation package.

The original audit ZIP is retained as a historical artifact, including its then-current bounded-uncertainty assessment. This notice supersedes that assessment. The active research question is whether the two surviving five-term families can be excluded by a reusable mechanism; no six-term theorem is claimed at this stage.
