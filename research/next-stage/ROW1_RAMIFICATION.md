# Proposed ramification obstruction for support {3,6,10,16,17,19}

Research only, 24 September 2026. Pending independent argument and coefficient audit. No manuscript or bundle update.

For the global exact-support conclusion, algebraic specialization precedes selection of a valuation: impose the rational CA common-root equations and inverse variables for each required nonzero coefficient. Any proper resulting ideal has an algebraic point over Qbar, preserving exact support. Select a 17-adic embedding of the number field generated by that point, then apply the established integral normalization and seed classification. This does not assert that an arbitrary complex point itself admits this embedding.

Normalize nu(17)=1. Assume a characteristic-zero CA candidate with exact normalized deficiency support S={3,6,10,16,17,19}, in the characteristic-17 row-1 seed fbar=X20-X3. The row-8 alternative was excluded previously. All coefficients are integral; the mean root is simple. Normalize the Hasse-third common root to one. Write t=a3, v=a6, b=a10, u=a16 and Cj=binom(20,j). Then

 f=X20+1140tX17+C6 vX14+C10 bX10+4845uX4+DX3+EX,
 D=-1140-775200t-364 C6 v-120 C10 b-19380u,
 E=-1-1140t-C6 v-C10 b-4845u-D.

Here f(1)=H3f(1)=0, D is a unit, E nonzero, t has positive valuation. The small cluster has zero plus two nonzero roots of equal valuation delta=nu(E)/2: in f(q)/q only E and Dq² can be minimal. It contains no repeated root, since f'(q)-f(q)/q has unique minimum 2Dq². Every repeated root belongs to the cluster at one.

G3=X³+t. Its selected root is nonzero and small because t is nonzero. Therefore nu(t)=3delta. All other contributions to E have valuation at least one. If delta<1/2, E would have value 2delta below both one and 3delta, impossible. Thus delta>=1/2 and nu(t)>=3/2.

## First divided condition

All coefficients c1,...,c16 in f(1+Y) belong to 17O, c17 is a unit. Any root displacement at one has value at least 1/16. Evaluating f' at a repeated root consequently gives f'(1)/17=0 in the residue field, exactly as in ROW1_UNIT16.md. The t contribution vanishes after division by 17. The complete binary witness recurrence for indices {6,10,16} leaves only residues (v,b,u)=(0,0,-1). This eight-choice assertion needs its explicit checker.

Now E/17 has residue 11: at t=v=b=0,u=-1 it is -788. Hence delta=1/2 and nu(t)=3/2 exactly. G6 and G10 select small roots. Their triangular recurrences give nu(v)>=3, nu(b)>=5. Contributions of v,b to ordinary f, D, E and derivatives have valuation at least four.

## Forced repeated-root collision

Let x be a G16 common root, necessarily reducing to one. Then

 u=-x16-560t x13+e, nu(e)>=3.

Write z=x-1. Direct expansion gives

 c1=17(-133-1425u)-1532160t+error(value>=4),
 c2=17(-190-1710u)-2170560t+error(value>=4),
 c3=0,
 c4=4845(1+u)+2713200t+error(value>=4).

The displayed t coefficients in c2,c4 are divisible by 17; c2/17 has residue 7. For k=5,...,16, ck lies in 17O, and c17=1140(1+t), c18=190,c19=20,c20=1.

If 0<epsilon=nu(z)<1/2, t has valuation 3/2 greater than 1+epsilon. In f(x)/z, the low linear-in-z sum has initial coefficient 10 after division by 17, while the order-17 term has value 16epsilon. All other terms are above the smaller of 1+epsilon and 16epsilon. Thus epsilon=1/15.

If epsilon>1/2, including z=0, u+1 has value greater than 1/2, so c1 has value exactly 3/2 from its t term. For finite epsilon>1/2 this is the unique smallest term in f(x)/z, impossible. If epsilon=1/2, c1 has value at least 3/2 and the divided-root equation forces value exactly 3/2, since c2 z has value 3/2 and every higher term has larger value. If z=0, c1 also has value exactly 3/2. In these latter cases the Newton polygon has fifteen simple outer roots with displacement value 1/15 (scaled nonzero roots of Y15+7), one simple root exactly one, and one simple inner root of displacement value 1/2. There is no repeated root, contradiction.

Thus epsilon=1/15. Choose pi with pi15=17. The complete initial polynomial for the seventeen-root unit cluster is

 L(Y)=Y17+7Y²+3xi Y, xi=bar((x-1)/pi), xi15=7.

For any unit root with displacement value eta<1/15, the c17 Y16 term in f(1+Y)/Y would be uniquely lowest. Thus the displayed initial polynomial captures all seventeen roots in the unit cluster. All its roots except xi are simple and xi has multiplicity two. As a repeated root must exist in this cluster, its exact equality to x follows by root counting. Hence f(x)=f'(x)=G16(x)=0.

## Elimination and ramification mismatch

After substituting u=-x16-560t x13 and discarding v,b, errors in f(x)/x and f'(x)-f(x)/x have valuation at least four. These expressions are P0(x)+t P1(x), Q0(x)+t Q1(x), where

 P0=-4844X19+19380X18-14535X16-1140X²+1139,
 Q0=-14516X19+38760X18-2280X²,
 P1=-2712060X16+10852800X15-8139600X13-775200X²+774060,
 Q1=-8121360X16+21705600X15-1550400X².

Q1(1)=16 mod17 is a unit. Put R=P0 Q1-Q0 P1. Its exact integer expansion at 1 has a factor z². The coefficient at z² has valuation one and divided residue 7; the coefficient at z17 has residue 16. For every coefficient r_k, nu(r_k)+(k-2)/15>=1, with equality only at k=2,17. These finite claims require an exact certificate.

Then S(Z)=R(1+pi Z)/(17 pi² Z²) is integral over Q17(pi), with reduction 7-Z15. Its reduction has simple roots. Let L be the degree-four unramified extension of Q17(pi). It contains every root of 7-Z15 in its residue field: 5^15=7 in F17 and 15 divides 17^4-1. In particular xi is such a root. Let Z0 in L be its unique Hensel lift, and x0=1+pi Z0. The ramification index of L over Q17 is 15.

For the actual Z=(x-1)/pi, elimination gives nu(R(x))>=4, hence nu(S(Z))>=43/15. The integral divided difference (S(Z)-S(Z0))/(Z-Z0) is a unit, because its reduction is S'(xi). Therefore nu(Z-Z0)>=43/15 and nu(x-x0)>=44/15. This argument remains valid in any valued common algebraic extension.

Set t0=-Q0(x0)/Q1(x0) in L. The integral rational function -Q0/Q1 is 1-Lipschitz on the residue class of one. The approximate Q equation gives nu(t-t0)>=min(4,44/15)=44/15>3/2. Since nu(t)=3/2, this implies nu(t0)=3/2. But L has value group (1/15)Z, which does not contain 3/2. Contradiction.

If all pending checks pass, row1 and hence the whole exact support S are excluded. This is not a claim that every seven-term support is excluded, nor a full degree-twenty proof.


## Proposed uniform extension (separate pending audit)

The same ramification argument should exclude a whole residue stratum, without a bound on the number of middle terms. Assume a2=a18=0 exactly; a3=t is nonzero with positive valuation; a4,...,a15 all have residue zero; a16 has residue -1; and fbar=X20-X3. Normalize the Hasse-third witness to one.

If E=0, zero is a triple root and exhausts the small cluster, whereas G3=X³+t with t nonzero requires a nonzero common root in that cluster. This is impossible. Thus E is nonzero. The zero cluster again consists of zero and two nonzero roots of value delta=nu(E)/2. G3 gives nu(t)=3delta, and E's formula forces delta>=1/2. The stipulated coefficient residues give E/17=11 in the residue field, hence delta=1/2 and nu(t)=3/2. Induction in the triangular equations now gives nu(aj)>=j/2 for every j=4,...,15, since all their selected roots reduce to zero. Their ordinary binomial multipliers have valuation one. Their aggregate contribution to f,D,E, and ordinary derivatives therefore has valuation at least three; their contribution to G16 has valuation at least two.

Replace the error threshold four by three throughout the collision and elimination proof. All collision arguments still hold because three exceeds every low order used there. The same P0,P1,Q0,Q1 and R are obtained after discarding these coefficients. The final bounds become

 nu(R(x))>=3,
 nu(S((x-1)/pi))>=28/15,
 nu(x-x0)>=29/15>3/2.

The value-group contradiction is unchanged. Thus this extension, if audited, excludes all such nonzero-a3 candidates, including cases with a4 nonzero. If a3=0, only the prior ROW1_UNIT16 theorem with a4=0 is currently invoked; no conclusion about a3=0,a4 nonzero is claimed. In the uniform extension the coefficient residues are hypotheses; the small census is needed only for the exact-support application.
