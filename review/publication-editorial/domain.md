## Findings on the statement and mathematical language

**D1 — Scope is correctly preserved.** Theorem 1 concerns a hypothetical nontrivial characteristic-zero CA polynomial of degree twenty, centered at the unique nineteenth-derivative root. It counts the leading monomial among the eight. The manuscript explicitly distinguishes deficiencies from exponents, and explains that multiplication of the variable by a nonzero scalar preserves exact support. It neither asserts the same count for arbitrary translations nor asserts that an eight-term counterexample exists. The abstract, introduction, restatement and closing scope section agree. They do not claim to prove the unrestricted degree-twenty conjecture, the general conjecture, or sharpness.

The normalization also treats the arbitrary characteristic-zero field quantifier responsibly. The exact support is enforced by an inverse-product equation before algebraic specialization; valuation normalization is chosen afterwards. The manuscript expressly avoids asserting preservation of a preselected valuation stratum under specialization. This is the appropriate order for an algebraic nonexistence proof.

**D2 — Derivative and support conventions are coherent.** Hasse derivatives are defined before positive-characteristic reduction. The normalized degree-j derivative G_j is distinguished from the Hasse derivative of order 20-j. The coefficient recurrence is justified before reducing binomial-normalized coefficients; merely being an ordinary integral coefficient would not suffice when the binomial multiplier is divisible by the chosen prime. Exact zeros, zero residues and selected common-root locations are kept distinct. In particular, the row-4 exact-zero restriction is used through a simple mean and a pure-zero gcd, not through coefficient reduction alone.

The closing table assigns an exclusion to all fourteen necessary supports and distinguishes the two separate branches of S_13 from its impossible row-4 alternative. The work on only some of the larger row-9 collection is explicitly outside the completed theorem's coverage claim. These are material safeguards, not just presentation details.

## Structural lemmas and their uses

**D3 — Lemma 3 is sound in the stated proof setting.** The argument uses an integral polynomial system over a valuation ring and an invertible reduced Jacobian. For a difference vector of minimum valuation r, an integral matrix with integral inverse preserves that minimum. The quadratic and higher Taylor terms have valuation at least 2r, while the residual has valuation at least q. If 0 < r < q, a linear coordinate of valuation r cannot cancel. No completeness, discrete value group or unramifiedness assumption is used in that argument.

The quadratic-coefficient section supplies the conditions this principle needs: it obtains the finite leading residue domain from the actual initial polynomial, omits coordinates already fixed exactly, identifies the unit diagonal and vanishing off-diagonal entries, and establishes residual precision before using a second expansion. It does not simply assume that all subsequent corrections occur at integral prime-adic orders. In particular, the nonsquare-discriminant step is made over F_17 only after proving that the relevant leading locations belong to F_17; it is not applied to arbitrary elements of its algebraic closure. The displayed contribution from the nineteenth Taylor term is retained.

**D4 — Lemma 4 has the necessary hypothesis and a valid proof.** Its force comes from requiring a selected common root for every Hasse order 1 through m-1 inside the separated m-root cluster. Mere occupancy by several roots would be insufficient. If the cluster does not collapse, choose a root as center and an element whose absolute value equals the greatest internal distance. The normalized cluster factor then has integral coefficients and at least two different residual roots. Strict separation places every outside root farther away. Normalizing the outside factor by its constant term makes its reduction a nonzero constant. In the Hasse product rule all its positive-order derivative contributions vanish on reduction, so every selected incidence descends to the degree-m cluster factor. This contradicts the assumed good-characteristic CA property.

This reasoning works after extending the valued field to contain the finitely many roots and the scale. It allows arbitrary ramification. In the row-2 application, the preceding initial model identifies thirteen simple outside branches and puts all three low-order witnesses into the remaining four-root cluster. The elementary characteristic-seventeen quartic argument then supplies exactly the small-degree hypothesis. The cubic use in Appendix B has the analogous sufficient hypothesis. The text does not claim that a good small degree alone settles a bad larger degree.

**Optional wording refinement, not a required repair:** In Lemma 3, “valuation ring” would be more precise than “valued ring.” In Lemma 4, one could explicitly define separation as every outside-to-cluster distance being strictly greater than the cluster diameter. The proofs and surrounding applications already use these meanings, so I do not regard either wording choice as a mathematical gap or a reason to hold this submission.

**D5 — Theorem 2's uniformity is correctly limited.** Its statement retains the exact conditions a_2=a_18=0, the characteristic-seventeen model, and the specified middle coefficient residues. It permits a_3 and the intermediate coefficients to vanish or remain nonzero within those conditions. The structural map and conclusion expressly identify uniformity in these coefficient choices at fixed degree twenty, not a theorem across degrees or all characteristic-seventeen strata. The subsequent case cover invokes it only after the appropriate residue and exact-zero conditions have been established. The manuscript does not equate this result with general Hensel lifting or with an unramified-only search.

## Novelty, significance and disposition

The manuscript properly places the established prime-adic, determinant, triangular and local-lifting machinery before its own claim. Lemmas 3 and 4 are presented as standard reusable reasoning, while the claimed contribution is the complete fixed-degree application and resulting eight-term bound. The result is substantially more definite than a list of favorable computations: it supplies a global coefficient-support restriction with a complete stated case cover and includes an argument about denser polynomials in one explicit stratum. This gives a credible specialist research contribution for an unrefereed preprint.

This review does not establish unconditional novelty. The stated ProofAtlas equivalence question and unavailable thesis full text remain real limitations; the manuscript says so. Nor does the review establish an external assessment grade, broad disciplinary importance, or acceptance by a specialist journal. No such claim appears in the reviewed manuscript. Subject to the separate methodology and publication-integrity decisions, I recommend **Accept**, with those qualifications visible and with this report retained as **internal, producer-coordinated domain review**.
