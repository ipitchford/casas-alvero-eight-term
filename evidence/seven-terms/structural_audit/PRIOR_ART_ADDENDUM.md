# Bounded fingerprint refresh for the degree-20 seven-term target

23 September 2026. This is a small update to the earlier reports, not a new
comprehensive literature review. No outreach or publication was performed.

Four fresh search queries were run:

1. `"Casas-Alvero" "20" "seven" "terms"`
2. `"Casas-Alvero" "six" "nonzero"`
3. `"Casas-Alvero" "x^{20}" "x^{16}" "x^{15}"`
4. `"Casas-Alvero" "4, 5, 10, 17, 19"`

No retrieved primary result stated the exact seven-total-term degree-20
bound or the remaining exact C support exclusion. This is an observation
about these queries and returned material, not proof of novelty. Secondary
aggregators and unrelated matches were not used as mathematical evidence.

The [current arXiv record for Marashdeh's *A descent-set obstruction for the
Casas–Alvero conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14726) still exposes only
v1, with submission history dated 12 August 2026. Its abstract advertises
the support reduction, a two-element-support result, and an at-least-four-
term consequence in centered normal form. That is not the proposed
degree-20 seven-term bound. Its detailed comparison and old-method
attributions remain in the earlier prior-art reports; this refresh does
not reread all of its proofs or establish that no implicit overlap exists.

The [ProofAtlas Casas–Alvero project
page](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/casas-alvero-conjecture/) remains
a material unresolved overlap lead. It discusses bounded degree-20
certificate work, Hensel expansion, affine mean-root equations, and
internal systems called A1/A2/A3. Its public record says that a complete A3
certificate audit and exact A1/A2 elimination remain tasks. The retrieved
page does not supply the support exponents, integer systems, or complete
certificates needed for a mathematical comparison with our C family. Its
labels must not be identified with this campaign's A/B/C labels. The
page is primary evidence of that project's reported activity, not an
independently established theorem.

The search again returned Gasull's [*A Primer on Resultants and Their
Applications*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44425-026-00047-6),
whose section 3.4 names degree 24 as the smallest open case. The previously
documented conflict with the primary Draisma–de Jong erratum and specialist
degree-20 sources is not resolved by this repeated search hit. No new
degree-20 proof was inferred from that sentence.

**Priority boundary:** the new valuation arguments are concrete arithmetic
lemmas using standard unit-Jacobian and least-valuation principles. No claim
that these proof techniques are new is warranted. The exact sparse bound
is a candidate contribution pending full assembly and stronger priority
review, especially resolution of the ProofAtlas source gap. The bounded
search found no exact collision; it did not clear that gap.
