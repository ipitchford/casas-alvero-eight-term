# Characteristic-zero exclusion of the support {4,10,17,19}

## Normalization and exact integral model

Let a centered degree 20 polynomial with precisely these nonleading support indices be

    f(x)=x^20+a*x^16+b*x^10+c*x^3+d*x,

with all four lower coefficients nonzero. The active Hasse derivative orders are16,10,3,1. Their constant terms are respectively a,b,c,d, so each requires a nonzero common root with f. All other derivative orders share 0 with f automatically. Choose the four witnesses, scale the first to 1, and denote the others u,v,w. Coincident witnesses are allowed.

The triangular derivative equations uniquely yield integer polynomials

    a=-4845,
    b=-184756*u^10-8008*a*u^6,
    c=-1140*v^17-560*a*v^13-120*b*v^7,
    d=-20*w^19-16*a*w^15-10*b*w^9-3*c*w^2.

Put P(X)=X^19+a*X^15+b*X^9+c*X^2+d, and define

    E1=P(1), Eu=P(u), Ev=P(v), Ew=P(w),
    I=(E1,Eu,Ev,Ew) in Z[u,v,w].

Any characteristic-zero CA polynomial of this support provides a zero of I. Consequently it suffices to prove I Q[u,v,w]=(1); no saturation is needed for this stronger conclusion.

## Exact finite certificate

Modulo13, b is identically zero because 13 divides both 184756 and 8008. Therefore E1,Ev,Ew depend only on v,w. The supplied mod13-certificate.txt contains three polynomials with respectively 481,649,647 terms satisfying

    C1*E1+C2*Ev+C3*Ew = 1 in F13[v,w].

The standalone verifier reconstructs the coefficients using FOUR formal derivative witnesses over Z, then normalizes the first to 1. It checks the generator match and the polynomial identity by exact standard-library arithmetic. It also checks deliberate corruption is rejected. Thus the ordinary ideal I modulo 13 is the unit ideal.

This fact alone is insufficient to deduce characteristic-zero emptiness. The next argument supplies the missing finiteness.

## Finiteness over Z_(13)

Let A=Z_(13), and set F=Eu-E1 and G=Ev-E1. These belong to Z[u,v] and have total degree 19. Their highest homogeneous parts, denoted F19 and G19, satisfy

    F19 = u^19+4*u^2*v^17 (mod13),
    G19 = 5*v^19 (mod13).

Every homogeneous monomial of degree 37 in F13[u,v] belongs to the homogeneous ideal generated by these two forms:

- if its v exponent is at least 19, use G19;
- if the v exponent is between 2 and 18, use F19 once to replace u^19 by-4*u^2*v^17, then use G19;
- if the v exponent is 0 or1, use F19 twice, then use G19.

The substitutions are legitimate: in the middle case the u exponent is at least 19; in the last case it starts at 37 or 36 and remains at least 19 after the first substitution. Each expression uses homogeneous multipliers of degree 18.

It follows that the degree 37 Macaulay map

    (H18)^2 -> H37,  (L,M) -> L*F19+M*G19,

is surjective modulo 13. Both domain and codomain have dimension 38. The integer matrix of this map is therefore invertible over A: its determinant is a unit. The verifier independently constructs the 38-by38 matrix from the original integer equations and computes determinant 5 modulo 13.

Hence, over A, each monomial u^i*v^j with i+j=37 is a homogeneous degree 18 combination ofF19,G19. Subtract the same combination ofF,G: the degree 37 parts cancel, leaving total degree at most 36. In the quotient by (F,G), every degree 37 monomial is consequently an A-linear combination of monomials of degree at most 36. Multiplying and reducing repeatedly shows that

    A[u,v]/(F,G)

is generated as an A-module by the 703 monomials of total degree at most 36.

Finally, W=Ew-E1 is monic of degree 19 in w, with coefficients inZ[u,v]. Thus

    A[u,v,w]/(F,G,W)

is generated by at most 703*19=13357 monomials as an A-module. Its quotientR=A[u,v,w]/I is also finite as an A-module.

## Transfer

The checked modular identity gives R/13R=0. Nakayama's lemma, applied to the finite module R over the local ringA, implies R=0. Extending scalars gives

    Q[u,v,w]/I Q[u,v,w]=0.

Therefore the marked root system has no complex solution, and no centered degree 20 CA counterexample has precisely the support {20,16,10,3,1}.

## Scope and assurance

The certificate proves a finite identity, and the checker verifies the exact integer-model and Macaulay hypotheses used above. The normalization, degree-reduction argument, and Nakayama implication are written mathematical proofs, not proof-assistant formalizations. This excludes one five-total-term family. Combining it with the separately audited support filters may close the centered five-term class; historical novelty and the full degree 20 conjecture remain separate questions.
